Method and sourcesHow the numbers are built

The verdict is GO when, in every year, cash available covers loan payments at least as many times as the bank's minimum cover. It is TIGHT when cover stays above 1.0 but dips below the bank's minimum in some year, and NO GO when any year's cash falls short of what is owed.

Cash available is rent from both buildings, less running costs and profit tax, plus the MCE operating cash you commit to the project. Loan payments are interest and principal on the new Building B loan plus payments on the existing building's remaining loan. The two buildings are treated as a separate real estate business with their own income statement. Profit tax is charged on rent after running costs, interest and depreciation, and losses can be carried forward five years if that option is on. MCE's cash is taken after the pay TV business has paid its own tax, so it is not taxed again.

The land is paid in cash at the start. The extra company cash funds the first part of construction, and the bank funds the rest as construction proceeds. The all-in finished cost per m² includes kitchens and appliances, and rises with construction inflation from today. Interest during construction is either paid from income or added to the loan. Repayment starts after the grace period and runs as equal monthly instalments over the loan term.

Rents are set in US dollars and collected in birr at the exchange rate of the day, which moves with the stated depreciation. After completion, Building B's rental income builds up evenly to full occupancy over the months it takes to fully rent. The worst case applies all the stresses in the Worst case group at once.

Default inputs come from the Building Project B workbook prepared by MCE finance (rentable area, apartment mix, dollar rents, existing loan of ETB 26.3M at 19 percent, MCE dividend capacity) and your note that land costs about ETB 83M plus fees and that finished construction costs ETB 100,000 per m². Bank rates reflect the NBE policy rate of 16 percent set in July 2026 (Briefs).
